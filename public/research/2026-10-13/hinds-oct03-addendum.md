## Rule facts and correction

PUCT Project 58481 adopted 16 TAC 25.194; effective 8 October 2026. Scope is ERCOT interconnection. The rule defines large-load customer using >=75 MW single-site expected non-coincident peak, with new-load and expansion applicability provisions. Co-location is also listed, but the October 2 alert's claim that it applies regardless of MW was overbroad. Read scope, applicability and definition together; obtain written interpretation for a sub-75 MW co-location proposal. Nonexporting backup is not automatically a co-located generation resource.

For qualifying requests, the intermediate agreement requires a $100,000 study fee and $50,000/MW security. An executed option can establish intermediate-stage site control. SLLIA site control requires a deed, qualifying lease, or executed PSA, and execution/funding is due within 60 days after allocation. At SLLIA, security is the greater of $50,000/MW or allocated system-upgrade costs; direct interconnection costs are cash CIAC. Existing queue agreements have no blanket grandfathering. Withdrawal, compliance and audit failures can cost security or capacity. Utility-specific and other applicable rules remain separate.

## Cheapest next verification sequence

1. Seller/broker: obtain the dated offered-tract polygon/legal description, existing power/fiber correspondence, applicant identity and authority to make utility inquiries. If no documents exist, retain UNKNOWN.
2. Serving-utility desk: request written identification of the authorized DSP/TSP, ISO/RTO and POI for that exact carve-out. Do not select a regime by a favorable map overlap.
3. Utility intake: submit a nonbinding question set for 25 facility MW and a genuine 50 MW expansion hypothesis, including hourly profile, target date and redundancy. Ask which process and financial/site-control obligations apply. No application, security or commitment is authorized.
4. TCEQ/permit specialist: inventory standby-generator air authorization, construction stormwater, wastewater, water and other actual design needs; ask what can be processed versus issued under the September directive.
5. Buyer/sponsor: establish maximum acceptable power price, delivery window, network requirements and the evidence milestone it will fund or pay to acquire. No interest is assumed.

## Questions that must be answered before paid control

- Is the offered tract sufficient and legally defined for the load facilities, substation, access and offsite rights?
- What peak and campus scope does the utility use, including phased additions and multiple meters?
- Which stage accepts an option, which requires a PSA/lease/deed, and how much time remains to satisfy it?
- Does changing applicant, ownership/control, load type, design, phase schedule or POI trigger consent, restudy, reset or forfeiture?
- Can the land option and utility position transfer to the same buyer on compatible dates? Will the utility affirmatively release Syncranicity and replace its security?
- Who pays each study/security/CIAC obligation, when, and what happens if the buyer withdraws? Seller refunds or diligence reimbursement do not automatically cover utility exposure.
- Can affordable control survive the funded milestone plus realistic delay buffer? If not, use free access/exclusivity or decline the structure.
