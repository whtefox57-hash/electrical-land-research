## The finding that matters most

**The Arizona Computer Data Center tax exemption is CLOSED to new applicants
until 1 July 2029.**

The FY2027 budget (HB 4168 / SB 1861), signed by Governor Hobbs on **13 June
2026** and effective **1 July 2026**, bars the Arizona Commerce Authority from
accepting applications to the Computer Data Center program from **1 July 2026
through 30 June 2029**. The bar applies to **renewals as well as new
applications**.

In the two weeks between signature and effect, the state received **113
applications — more than the 83-odd approved in the program's prior 13 years
combined.**

Why this matters to you commercially: for the next three years, any developer
you sell or lease to cannot obtain new CDC certification, so the sales-and-use
tax exemption is off the table unless they already hold it. That shifts value
toward **land whose buyer is already certified**, and it removes a subsidy that
had been partly capitalised into Arizona land prices. It also tells you which way
the political wind is blowing at state level.

- https://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2026/07/09/data-centers-dominated-2026-session-lawmakers-answered-with-3-year-tax-incentive-pause/
- https://www.azfamily.com/2026/06/10/arizona-budget-deal-includes-3-year-pause-data-center-tax-exemptions/
- https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report-state/arizona-data-center-tax-incentive-pause-signed-by-governor-hobbs
- https://www.axios.com/local/phoenix/2026/07/08/data-center-tax-break-applications-double-moratorium

### Underlying program thresholds — A.R.S. § 41-1519

These remain the statutory tests; they are simply unavailable to new applicants
until 2029.

| County population | Minimum new investment | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **more than 800,000** | **$50,000,000** | Maricopa, Pima |
| **800,000 or less** | **$25,000,000** | every other Arizona county |

Investment counts land, buildings, improvements, modular data centers and
computer data center equipment. Relief flows through A.R.S. §§ 42-5061, 42-5159
and 42-6004, and extends to qualified colocation tenants.

- https://www.azleg.gov/ars/41/01519.htm
- https://www.azcommerce.com/incentives/computer-data-center-program/

**Practical consequence for site selection:** the $25M tier covers all 13
non-metro counties. A site in Pinal, Yavapai, Mohave, Cochise, Yuma, Navajo,
Apache, Coconino, Gila, Graham, Greenlee, La Paz or Santa Cruz clears the
investment bar at half the Maricopa/Pima figure — relevant again from July 2029.

## Utility large-load thresholds

There is no single statewide MW trigger. Each utility sets its own, and the
regulatory path differs sharply depending on who serves the parcel.

### APS — ACC-regulated

| Item | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Rate Schedule **XHLF** (General Service Extra High Load Factor) | **≥ 5,000 kW (5 MW)** monthly maximum demand **and ≥ 92% load factor** | Existing tariff |
| Rate Schedule **E-34** | Extra Large General Service | Existing tariff |
| Rate Schedule **E-32 L** | Large General Service | Existing tariff |

**XHLF is in active revision.** APS's June 2025 rate case (**Docket
E-01345A-25-0105**) proposes changed eligibility criteria and minimum-bill
requirements for XHLF, plus direct assignment of new generation costs to
extra-large users — reported as roughly a **45% increase for that class**.
Hearings opened **18 May 2026**; a decision is expected **before end of 2026**.

**Do not quote XHLF terms to a developer as settled.** The eligibility floor
itself is one of the things under revision.

- https://www.aps.com/-/media/APS/APSCOM-PDFs/Utility/Regulatory-and-Legal/Regulatory-Plan-Details-Tariffs/Business/Business-NonResidential-Plans/ExtraHighLoadFactor.pdf
- https://www.aps.com/en/Utility/Regulatory-and-Legal/Rate-case
- https://dailyenergyinsider.com/news/51076-aps-moves-to-update-data-center-rates-as-demand-surges-across-arizona/

### SRP — public power, NOT ACC-regulated

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| **E-67** commercial/industrial plan, large-customer provision | New large accounts with **≥ 20 MW forecast load** face minimum billing: the greater of actual demand or **80% of forecast load** |
| Approved | SRP Board, **27 February 2025** |

SRP's board is **elected by landowners within the district**, on an acreage-based
franchise, not by the ACC and not by general electorate. A large-load
conversation in SRP territory is a conversation with SRP staff and ultimately
that board — there is no ACC docket to intervene in.

- https://www.srpnet.com/price-plans/electric-pricing-public-process/overview-commercial-changes
- https://www.srpnet.com/about/data-center-facts

### TEP — ACC-regulated

TEP has no published generic large-load MW threshold I could confirm. What TEP
has instead is a **precedent**: the ACC approved a **special agreement for
electric service** for Project Blue on **3 December 2025** by **4–1**, capped at
**286 MW**, at full approved rates with no discount, with the customer bearing
the cost of any incremental generation beyond that cap.

That is the template for how a large load gets served in TEP territory: a
bespoke, docketed special contract, not a standard tariff election.

**It is also being litigated.** Attorney General Mayes sued the ACC over the
TEP–Project Blue agreement and has since filed an appeal.

- https://www.azcc.gov/news/home/2025/12/05/acc-approves-tep-energy-supply-agreement--ensures-customer-protections-from-costs-related-to-data-center-project
- https://www.tucsonsentinel.com/local/report/021926_mayes_tep_rate/ag-mayes-sues-corp-comm-over-tep-project-blue-electric-deal
- https://tucson.com/news/local/government-politics/article_74cb122e-b026-4290-a88f-6a489070d309.html

### ACC posture generally

The Commission held a **Data Center / Large Load Workshop** and published
highlights on **20 April 2026**. The direction of travel across both APS and TEP
proceedings is consistent: **large loads pay their own way, and the Commission is
building explicit cost-shift protections for other ratepayers.**

- https://www.azcc.gov/news/home/2026/04/20/acc-large-load-data-center-workshop-highlights

## Municipal thresholds and standards

### Phoenix — ordinance ADOPTED

Adopted **2 July 2025** (text amendment Z-TA-2-25-Y).

- Defines "Data Center". Carve-out: not a data center if it occupies **< 10% of
  gross floor area** of on-site buildings, serves only the owner's own
  enterprise, and leases no services to third parties.
- Permitted in **C-2, C-3, CP/GCP, A-1, A-2** — **only with a Special Permit**.
- Performance standards include **half-mile spacing from approved high-capacity
  transit**, a **noise study and mitigation** where within **300 feet** of a
  residentially zoned district, and a **will-serve letter from the utility**.

The will-serve requirement is the operative one for you: it converts "there is a
substation nearby" into "the utility has committed in writing", which is exactly
the gap the Texas screen could never close.

- https://www.phoenix.gov/newsroom/pdd-news/city-of-phoenix-updates-zoning-to-safeguard-health-and-safety-as.html
- https://www.phoenix.gov/content/dam/phoenix/pddsite/documents/staffreports/z-ta-2-25-y-data-center.pdf

Phoenix is nonetheless **facing legal pressure over the new rules** and has
**delayed at least one data center rezone vote** in 2026.

- https://www.azfamily.com/2026/03/26/phoenix-faces-growing-legal-pressure-over-new-data-center-rules/
- https://azbex.com/legislation-issues/phoenix-delays-data-center-rezone-vote/

### Tucson — ordinance DRAFTED, not yet adopted

A Unified Development Code amendment for **Large-Scale Data Centers** has been in
process since a Planning Commission memo dated **17 September 2025**.

- Would require all large-scale data centers to sit under **PAD or PCD zoning**,
  forcing Mayor and Council review of each one.
- Would govern location, building size and setbacks.
- Would require the developer to **publicly document sufficient water and power**.
- Planning Commission moved it to public hearing on **3 June 2026**; it has since
  advanced to Mayor and Council for possible adoption.

**A moratorium was discussed and not pursued** — the city attorney advised it
could expose Tucson legally and "doesn't have the power that most people think a
moratorium would have."

The widely repeated "25,000 sq ft or 20 MW" figure appears in coverage of the
draft. **I could not confirm those exact numeric triggers in the current adopted
text, because there is no adopted text.** Treat the thresholds as unsettled.

- https://www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Planning-Development-Services/Planning-Initiatives/Data-Centers-Unified-Development-Code-Amendment
- https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/pdsd/documents/boards-committees-commissions/planning-commission/09.17.25/pc-memo-_data-center-code-update-9-17-25.pdf
- https://www.kold.com/2026/05/07/tucson-planning-commission-reviews-proposed-data-center-regulations/

### Buckeye — no ordinance threshold; control exercised by development agreement

Buckeye's instrument is not a zoning threshold but a **contractual water cap**.
The Tract Buckeye Technology Park — **2,069 acres, up to 40 data centers, 1.8 GW
at build-out** — carries a **2,000 acre-feet per year cap written into the
development agreement**. The land's prior residential entitlement (~9,700 homes)
would have used roughly 4,500 AF/yr, which is the comparison the city leads with.

Tract intends to **self-supply by drilling groundwater wells** on site and later
transfer the system to the city.

- https://www.buckeyeaz.gov/business-development/economic-development/data-centers-fact-vs-fiction
- https://grist.org/technology/arizona-water-data-centers-semiconducters/

## How I would set `size_class` once candidates exist

Proposed buckets, keyed to thresholds that actually exist rather than an
invented scale:

| size_class | Definition | Triggers crossed |
|---|---|---|
| `sub_tariff` | < 5 MW | below APS XHLF floor |
| `xhlf_eligible` | 5–19.9 MW | APS XHLF (if ≥92% load factor) |
| `srp_minimum_bill` | 20–99.9 MW | SRP E-67 minimum billing; Tucson draft threshold if adopted |
| `large_load` | 100–285 MW | bespoke utility negotiation territory |
| `project_blue_scale` | ≥ 286 MW | at or above the only ACC-approved AZ special contract to date |

`capacity_basis` must name the source field it came from. Where a substation
publishes **MVA**, record MVA and **do not silently convert** — the power factor
is unknown and assuming 1.0 overstates servable MW.

## APPENDED 2026-07-29 — note from the GIS build (Steps 2–10)

**Do not edit above this line.** This note qualifies the `size_class` proposal
at the end of this document; it does not contradict the thresholds themselves,
which were all confirmed usable as written.

**The `size_class` scheme could only be applied to 7 of 255 candidates.**

The proposal assumed a capacity figure would exist per candidate. For retired
plants it does: EIA-860 2024 publishes `Nameplate Capacity (MW)` per unit, and
those 7 sites classify cleanly (3 `project_blue_scale`, 1 `large_load`, and the
rest below).

For the 248 substation candidates it does not. **The HIFLD substation schema
contains no capacity field of any kind** — the columns are NAME, TYPE, STATUS,
LINES, MAX_VOLT, MIN_VOLT plus administrative metadata. There is no MVA rating
and no MW rating.

This means the instruction in this document — "where a substation publishes
MVA, record MVA and do not silently convert" — **could not be exercised,
because no MVA is published anywhere in the acquired data.** The guidance was
right; the data simply does not reach it.

Those 248 candidates therefore carry `est_capacity_mw = null`,
`size_class = NOT_DETERMINED`, `capacity_confidence = low`, and a
`capacity_basis` string explaining the gap. Deriving MW from kV was rejected as
fabrication: servable load depends on transformer bank size, conductor rating
and the utility's own planning limits, none of which are public.

**Consequence for the screen:** the ranking measures *access* to the grid
(proximity, voltage, line counts) and not *room* on it. Resolving capacity
requires one call per site to the serving utility's system planning group.
