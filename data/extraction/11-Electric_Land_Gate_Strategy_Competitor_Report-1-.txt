ELECTRIC LAND GATE STRATEGY
Competitor map, capital deployment, customer acquisition, founder positioning, and 
scale plan
Prepared August 29, 2026 | Synchronicity / Electric Land concept
This brief gives the competitor analysis the most depth and keeps the operating recommendations concise. The strategic 
premise is that Synchronicity should enter as a service and evidence-orchestration company, not as an engineering firm, 
broker, or speculative land fund.
Executive conclusion
 Competition exists at almost every gate. Paces and LandGate already automate parcel screening and diligence; DC 
Byte sells market/infrastructure intelligence; major engineering firms perform technical diligence; global brokerages 
combine site selection, power, transactions and capital markets; Tract actually acquires and conditions powered land.
 The opening is narrower: own the cross-disciplinary decision record. Define the buyer requirement, kill false 
positives, route specialist questions, preserve provenance/transferability, monitor evidence expiration, and identify 
the cheapest next proof.
 The Arizona screen shows the problem: capacity was unknown for 248/255 candidates, utility assignment was 
ambiguous for 181/255, candidates were infrastructure points rather than confirmed parcels, and high water scores 
measured regulatory freedom rather than physical water.
 Do not rent an office or hire employees to manufacture credibility. Add fixed cost only when recurring work makes it 
operationally useful.

Founder myth without dishonesty
The useful version of "fake it until you make it" is not pretending to have experience, clients, capacity or credentials. It is 
adopting the operating behavior, presentation standards and risk discipline of the company you intend to become before 
you have its scale.
 Build the myth around a true obsession: "I started Synchronicity after discovering that the most valuable powered-
land facts were exactly the facts public datasets could not prove."
 Use a visible doctrine: Signals are not proof. Kill weak sites early. Every claim has a source. Every unknown has a 
next verification step.
 Borrow credibility transparently: an advisory bench of named licensed experts, paid per engagement. Say "reviewed 
by" only when it was actually reviewed.
 Publish narrow, technically careful field notes. Corrections increase credibility when made promptly.
 Look institutional through process: domain email, concise website, standard proposal, branded Passport, secure data 
room, written methodology, version control, conflicts policy, professional insurance, prompt follow-up.
 Do not fabricate an office, team, customer logos, utility relationships, partnerships, proprietary capacity, case studies, 
or testimonials. Do not call a contractor an employee or adviser without agreement.

Office rule: do not use rent as a credibility prop. A customer should infer seriousness from the work product, expert 
bench and evidence discipline. A two-employee Tucson office is reasonable around the small-firm stage if both people are 
local and customer/vendor meetings justify it. A 10-100 person facility belongs after product-market fit, not before it.
How to sell the company at every stage
 Lead with the customer loss you prevent: false-positive sites, duplicated diligence, stale evidence and capital spent 
before fatal flaws are known.
 Ask: "What evidence would your investment committee need before it treats this site as real?" Then build backward 
from that answer.
 For an inexperienced founder objection: "Do not underwrite my biography. Audit the process, the sources and the 
specialists responsible for each conclusion. Start with a fixed-scope pilot."
 For the competitor objection: "We do not replace your engineer, broker or lawyer. We make sure you hire the right 
one at the right time and that their work remains usable in the next decision."
 For the middleman objection: "A middleman passes introductions. We change the decision state: requirements 
become gates, claims become evidence, unknowns become verification tasks, and weak sites die before expensive 
diligence."
 For the speculative-startup objection: "The company begins as a cash-generating service. Software and land capital 
follow only after customers repeatedly pay for the workflow."
Capital deployment rule
Every dollar should purchase one of four assets: customer learning, proprietary verified information, repeatable 
workflow, or distribution. If it purchases appearance alone, defer it. Early capital should therefore favor expert reviews, 
data, travel to high-value calls, professional insurance, secure information systems and paid pilots. Land options, custom 
software, large offices and full-time specialists come later.
Research notes and sources
Project-file basis. The Arizona screening files report 255 evaluated candidates, 166 on private land, unknown substation 
capacity for 248 candidates, ambiguous overlapping utility territories for 181 candidates, and a point-not-parcel 
limitation. The water notes expressly warn that a 20/20 score means regulatory freedom rather than physical water. The 
viability report argues that value premiums attach primarily to entitlements and utility commitments, not consultant 
reports. These findings informed the recommended evidence-first model.
