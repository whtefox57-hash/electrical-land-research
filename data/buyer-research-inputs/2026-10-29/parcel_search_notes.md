## Decision

The most useful new low-cost discovery lane is smaller South Carolina industrial property, where public economic-development pages disclose utility providers, zoning and sometimes subdivision sizes. The best first action is to test whether a named regional developer actually wants this geography and phase size, then ask about the exact site. A rural Texas inference/AI buyer and a Columbia-area enterprise-colo buyer are solving different problems; neither should receive the same property pitch.

Potential first discovery comparisons:

1. **Dickert Rail Site, Newberry, SC — 70 acres, $12,000–15,000/acre.** Industrial zoning, water/sewer and AT&T fiber are publicly reported. Power shown is 12kV distribution, not committed 10–50MW. Compare a small regional/modular development brief for DC BLOX, DartPoints or GIGA, only if their current territory/customer needs validate it. The implied whole-site land range is $840,000–1.05 million, calculated rather than a single seller quote. Source: [Newberry County](https://www.newberrycounty.gov/property/dickert-rail-site), `turn87view0`.
2. **I-26 Public Works Site, Newberry, SC — 65 acres; minimum 20 acres; $15,000/acre.** A 20-acre phase has arithmetic land cost of $300,000, but parcel geometry, seller subdivision consent and extension rights are unknown. Telecom is “to suit”; utility capacity is unproven. This is a better founder-scale control hypothesis than automatically pursuing an entire institutional campus. Source: [Newberry County](https://www.newberrycounty.gov/property/i-26-public-works-site), `turn87view1`.
3. **Fish Hatchery Road, Pine Ridge, SC — 53 acres, $350,000.** Cheapest new explicit land-price lead, but also weak infrastructure evidence. Nearby residential/commercial use and untested wetland/flood/net-pad constraints make the first step a cheap fatal screen. This should not outrank a proven utility path because it is inexpensive. Source: [Central SC](https://www.centralsc.org/properties/fish-hatchery-road-site), `turn54view0`.

These are research priorities, not recommendations to pay for control. The original portal availability dates are old; continued public advertising must be confirmed with the current owner/broker.

## Common cheapest next proof

For every developer/site pair, ask the developer function: “Would you consider this market for a first phase of [defined MW and workload], and what minimum fiber, energization date, total cost, redundancy, net land and customer/lease evidence would make it reviewable?” This is discovery, not a claim that the site has power. Then obtain the owner-authorized utility request and exact parcel evidence. A public expansion announcement does not show that any developer wants one of these properties.

A build forecast conditional only on suitable power must still retain uncertainty for land control, zoning, environment, fiber, water/cooling, engineering/capex, customer demand, financing, timing and competition with the developer's existing controlled sites. Do not use “all diligence good” as a hidden extra assumption when the user only conditioned on power.
