# Independent matching review — 29 October 2026

Reviewed `sites_major.json` (5 institutional broker leads), `parcel_candidates.json` (17 parcel/park leads), and the available major/emerging/energy developer profiles. This review makes no new claim of seller, utility or developer engagement.

## Finding

The research supports a differentiated **discovery pipeline**, not 100 qualified acquisition mandates or calibrated construction probabilities. The institutional listings contain more explicit infrastructure/development claims; the cheaper sites offer more plausible founder-scale land economics. Neither establishes an assignable option or a developer's unmet need. The website should preserve that distinction in its first screen.

A meaningful match requires: (1) the buyer's evidenced business lane; (2) geography/network reach; (3) first phase and ultimate size; (4) development or conversion capability; (5) a reason to choose this site over already controlled inventory; and (6) a transaction route the founder can execute. The current cards are strongest on1–4 and mostly unproven on5–6.

## Matching corrections and cautions

- **Do not equate all company projects with acquisition criteria.** A612-acre QTS campus or1.4GW Vantage campus is a scale observation; it does not prove a universal minimum. Conversely, it is not evidence they want a standalone20MW site.
- **Do not transfer identity or mandates between similar names.** Emerging research replaced old GIGA Data Centers with distinct Giga Energy. I removed the stale GIGA matches from the17-site file, rather than moving the same Carolinas leads to Giga Energy without geography evidence.
- **Regional platforms still need demand.** The low-priced Newberry sites become buyer-first only after a developer explains a coverage, customer or network gap. Industrial zoning plus a state-level footprint is insufficient.
- **Existing network/campus adjacency can be negative.** The buyer may already have enough owned capacity, avoiding both a new market launch and an assignment premium.
- **Whole-property value is not control cost.** Land advertised for$350k can still have unaffordable hard-deposit terms; a much larger site could allow cheap exclusivity. Neither is established publicly.
- **Remove missing data from numeric comparisons.** Metamora's total-price and per-acre fields conflict; `askingPrice` is now null, with both figures retained in `quotedPriceText`. Do not sort it by an apparently settled price.
- **Tupelo's regional fiber proxy is not a measured lateral.** The broker reports service around Atoka about19miles away but does not establish the route required to serve this tract. Corrected wording avoids converting that distance into a construction estimate.

## Percentages: what can be said honestly

**No reviewed source supplies the denominator, comparable failed projects, live acquisition mandate, exact transferable control or validated commercial assumptions required for a calibrated probability that a particular developer will build on one of these properties.** Primary announcements are selected successes. Public listings are sales materials. Power suitability removes one constraint; it does not establish customer demand, land control, competitive position or financing.

Recommended presentation for the requested percentages:

1. Define the event: **the named developer starts physical construction of a specified first data-center phase on this exact tract by29October2031**. Do not mix this with operational energization, purchasing the option, or paying Syncranicity.
2. Define the user's condition narrowly: **sufficient technically deliverable capacity for that phase is confirmed**. Unless expressly assumed, tariff, upgrade cost, date, redundancy, fuel and transferability remain unresolved. Do not silently turn “suitable power capacity” into “all diligence good, tenant secured, funded and approved.”
3. Show **Actual probability: not yet estimable** alongside an optional **Analyst planning scenario: X–Y%**, with the latter visibly labeled uncalibrated and assumption-driven. No decimal percentages; no “confidence interval” label.
4. A scenario range must expose its ingredients. Use conditional branches, not an unqualified fit score: P(current mandate selects this site | power condition) × P(control, permits, fiber, cooling and economics clear | selected, power condition) × P(financed construction starts by horizon | prior conditions). Shared risks belong in coherent scenarios; the conditional chain does not assume independent marginal events.
5. Example of method only: an assumed10–25% site-selection chance,60–80% non-power execution chance given selection, and70–90% funded-start chance given the preceding steps produces about4–18%. These inputs are **not observed rates**, not estimates for any named buyer, and should not be auto-populated across100 firms. They illustrate how additional assumptions create a planning range; sources alone do not justify choosing them.
6. Prefer scenario sliders or an expandable assumption panel. Record baseline assumptions and “what changes this range.” Buyer validation, current utility documents, exact parcel control and an anchor-customer requirement should move the model; press-release volume should not.
7. Mark unresolved identity/status cases **HOLD / no forecast**. Do not rank Hinds or Old Wire as clean active opportunities, and do not attach a persuasive nonzero figure to an inactive listing merely because a hypothetical power condition was supplied. A hypothetical reoffering would be a new scenario.
8. For a company with no substantiated US acquisition mandate, label **No qualified US match**. A forced candidate and tiny invented percentage would weaken the exercise.

If the UI cannot show actual-vs-scenario distinctions clearly, displaying “Not estimable yet” is more honest than presenting a single fabricated chance. The deliverable can still satisfy the reverse-engineering objective with specific company requirements, ranked tests, candidate matches and explicit numerical scenarios for reviewed pairs.
